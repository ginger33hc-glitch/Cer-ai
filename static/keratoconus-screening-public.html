Keratoconus screening for ophthalmologists

Keratoconus screening software using Pentacam tomography

CER-AI supports ophthalmologists reviewing manifest refraction and five Pentacam images for keratoconus screening. Results: negative, suspicious or positive.

Enter the patient’s name, age and manifest refraction, then upload five Pentacam images: right and left Four Maps Refractive, right and left Belin/Ambrosio Display, and one Show 2 Exams Topometric report.

Unreadable measurements are requested from the surgeon. Screening uses the entered patient name and age; no LASIK, PRK or SMILE procedure selection is required.

Results are shown for each eye as negative, suspicious or positive. A PDF report can be opened, downloaded and shared after screening is completed.

Screening is clinical decision support. A positive result is not a confirmed keratoconus diagnosis; a negative result does not exclude disease. An ophthalmologist must interpret the findings.

What is screened? · Clinical workflow · Interpreting results · Questions

What is keratoconus screening?

Keratoconus screening is the clinician-led evaluation of corneal shape, thickness distribution and other clinical findings to identify eyes that warrant further investigation. Corneal topography maps anterior curvature; tomography also provides information about posterior elevation and pachymetric distribution. A screening result is not equivalent to a confirmed diagnosis.

For refractive surgery candidates, suspected subclinical or forme fruste keratoconus requires careful review before LASIK or other corneal laser procedures. Screening should take into account history, refraction, ocular surface and contact-lens effects, scan quality, and whether measurements are reproducible.

How does the CER-AI keratoconus screening workflow work?

  1. Obtain corneal measurements: an eye-care professional performs the examination and acquires Pentacam imaging with appropriate quality checks.
  2. Review source documents: the ophthalmologist enters manifest refraction and supplies five supported Pentacam report images.
  3. Resolve incomplete measurements: unreadable or conflicting required fields are flagged for physician confirmation rather than treated as normal.
  4. Inspect independent findings: the per-eye report displays the Randleman/ERSS topography component (not a full surgical ERSS score), Pentacam Final BAD-D classification, CER-AI-adapted NICE component scoring, and PS3 factor details.
  5. Interpret and follow up: the ophthalmologist reviews any suspicious findings in the clinical context; serial examinations may be necessary to evaluate progression.

AI assistance is used to read supplied report images. The subsequent screening classification is handled by a separate rule-based clinical engine. CER-AI does not acquire scans, manufacture an unreadable BAD-D value or independently diagnose keratoconus.

How should negative, suspicious and positive results be interpreted?

Negative: no alert was triggered under the completed screening rules, but keratoconus is not excluded. Suspicious: the findings require closer physician review or additional assessment. Positive: a screening alert was triggered; this alone is not confirmation of keratoconus. Not assessable: critical data are missing or unresolved, so the system cannot issue a completed screening classification.

Progression is a separate question. A single screening assessment cannot establish progression. Comparing dated, quality-verified serial tomography, refraction and clinical findings is necessary. CER-AI-specific diagnostic sensitivity and specificity have not been independently validated and are not claimed here.

Frequently asked clinical questions

Can keratoconus be ruled out by a normal Pentacam result?

No single index, scan or software label rules out keratoconus in every clinical setting. Examination, acquisition quality and follow-up remain important.

Is keratoconus screening the same as surgical ectasia risk assessment?

No. Keratoconus screening reviews corneal findings. Surgical ectasia risk assessment additionally requires procedure-specific considerations such as tissue safety. See corneal ectasia risk assessment before refractive surgery.

Who can use CER-AI screening?

The professional module is intended for ophthalmologists with authorized access. It is not an at-home keratoconus test or a substitute for an eye examination.

Clinical evidence / Klinik kanıtlar

Screening reports: what is explained?

The current report shows the findings behind each eye’s screening result: the Randleman/ERSS topography component, the measured Final BAD-D, the CER-AI-adapted NICE component values and points, and the PS3 factors classified as Moderate or High. In this screening module, the topography component is not the full ERSS surgical score.

What happens if a measurement cannot be read?

The workflow identifies the required field and asks the surgeon to review the source and complete it. Missing decision-critical information is not treated as a negative screening result. Upload, image reading, verification and report completion are shown as processing stages; the progress percentage is not a disease probability.

Does the report show keratoconus progression?

The report includes physician guidance on reviewing progression, but a single screening assessment does not establish progression. It directs the clinician to compare date-verified serial tomography, refraction and examination findings. Positive screening alone is not evidence of progression.

For procedure-specific tissue planning, use refractive surgery assessment. For cataract planning, see IOL selection and calculation.
