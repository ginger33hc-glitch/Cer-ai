Active clinical use at Vizyon Eye Hospital, Mersin

CER-AI is actively used at Vizyon Eye Hospital in Mersin, Türkiye, during patient examinations and surgical planning. It supports AI-assisted assessment for refractive surgery (laser vision correction, known in Turkish as “göz çizdirme”) and intraocular lens selection (“akıllı mercek”) for cataract surgery. Clinical decisions, final lens selection and surgery remain the responsibility of the ophthalmic surgeon.

CER-AI is designed to connect Pentacam-based corneal assessment with lens-category eligibility, toric-need assessment, the surgeon’s preferred lens family, and spherical IOL power calculation in one auditable workflow.

Which IOL category is suitable?

The IOL calculation and selection workflow first reviews the clinical and optical inputs for monofocal, enhanced monofocal, EDOF and multifocal eligibility. It then considers toric need. The surgeon decides which IOL and lens family to use; eligibility support does not choose a lens or establish a patient-specific prescription on its own.

The integrated workflow

1 · UploadUpload the defined Pentacam sources and required biometry report.
2 · AssessEvaluate optical and clinical eligibility for monofocal, enhanced monofocal, EDOF, or multifocal categories.
3 · RouteDetermine whether the measured regular corneal astigmatism requires toric planning.
4 · ChooseThe surgeon selects the preferred lens family; CER-AI does not make this choice autonomously.
5 · CalculateCalculate spherical IOL power with Cooke K6 when the required inputs are complete, then continue to toric planning when indicated.

Why this workflow is distinct

IOL power calculators and toric calculators commonly perform calculation after measurements and lens choices are supplied. CER-AI is designed to place the preceding lens-category and toric-need decision in the same traceable workflow as the calculation. This is a description of CER-AI’s architecture, not a claim that competing systems lack IOL or toric calculation.

Clinical boundary: CER-AI supports—not replaces—surgeon judgment. Source-image quality, contraindications, ocular examination, biometric plausibility, lens availability, constants, and the final lens decision remain the surgeon’s responsibility.
