Independent ectasia assessment platform — currently free access

CER-AI is a standalone web platform for corneal ectasia risk assessment before LASIK and PRK (göz çizdirme), including review of risk factors associated with post-LASIK ectasia. It runs outside the imaging device's software.

Device-associated tools include the Belin/Ambrósio Display (BAD) in the Pentacam software ecosystem and the SCORE Analyzer on the ANTERION imaging platform. Access to these tools requires the relevant imaging system and applicable software configuration or licensing; equipment and license costs vary by supplier and configuration.

CER-AI adds a separate workflow using existing supported Pentacam reports. It does not replace tomography hardware or generate BAD-D without the device report. Current compatibility is defined for Pentacam sources; support for every imaging device is not claimed.

Current access is free for approved physicians. New demo accounts have a 10-patient allowance and require approval. A low-cost physician subscription is planned for the future; no final tariff or start date has been announced.

Laser vision correction: what does “göz çizdirme” mean?

“Göz çizdirme” is the everyday Turkish term for laser vision correction, including LASIK and PRK. CER-AI supports AI-assisted preoperative assessment before laser eye surgery: Pentacam image reading, keratoconus and ectasia risk review, and corneal tissue-safety checks.

Does AI-assisted göz çizdirme mean AI performs the operation?

No. The phrase “yapay zekâ destekli göz çizdirme” here refers to decision support before LASIK or PRK. CER-AI organizes the assessment; the qualified ophthalmic surgeon makes the clinical decision and performs surgery.

CER-AI is web-based corneal ectasia screening and risk assessment software for qualified ophthalmologists and refractive surgeons. It supports structured preoperative review before LASIK and PRK, with AI-assisted reading of Pentacam images and separately implemented assessment rules.

Clinical problem addressed

Postoperative corneal ectasia is a major safety concern in corneal refractive surgery. Preoperative screening therefore examines multiple domains rather than relying on a single measurement. These domains can include anterior corneal topography, corneal tomography, pachymetry, refractive magnitude, age, residual stromal bed and other procedure-specific factors.

Corneal ectasia screening before refractive surgery

For a refractive surgeon, a corneal ectasia risk assessment is not simply a search for an abnormal topography label. It asks whether the complete preoperative evidence supports proceeding with LASIK or PRK, changing the proposed procedure, obtaining better-quality or longitudinal data, or deferring treatment.

CER-AI is designed for this physician workflow. It organizes preoperative ectasia screening across corneal topography and tomography, pachymetry, refractive and age-related factors, independent ERSS, BAD-D, NICE and PS3 pathways, and procedure-specific tissue safety. Pentacam ectasia screening is therefore one evidence source within the assessment—not a replacement for clinical examination or surgeon judgment.

Post-LASIK ectasia risk assessment and keratoconus screening review both depend on the quality and interpretation of preoperative evidence. CER-AI helps the surgeon examine susceptibility before surgery; it does not diagnose keratoconus or predict an individual patient's postoperative outcome.

Clinicians evaluating LASIK ectasia risk can review the corneal ectasia screening guide, examine worked synthetic cases, and trace the underlying claims through the clinical evidence map and medical reference registry.

What should surgeons check when evaluating software for ectasia assessment?

Check whether the software shows its measurement sources, keeps risk pathways interpretable, identifies missing or conflicting values, separates tissue safety from ectasia susceptibility, and explains its validation limits. These are features to review alongside clinical evidence and the surgeon's own examination.

How does CER-AI use artificial intelligence?

AI assists with reading information from supplied Pentacam images. Extracted measurements can be incorrect, incomplete or conflicting; source review and correction remain essential parts of professional use.

Clinical risk classification and tissue-safety calculations are handled separately by the canonical rule-based assessment engine. AI-assisted image reading is not a claim that CER-AI is an externally validated autonomous diagnostic or ectasia-prediction model.

Final BAD-D is read from the Pentacam display. CER-AI does not acquire the corneal scan, replace the Pentacam device or reconstruct its proprietary indices.

Risk pathways represented in CER-AI

Randleman / ERSS

The Randleman Ectasia Risk Score System is represented as an independent clinical risk pathway using its defined component structure and anterior-topography evidence.

Final BAD-D

Pentacam Belin/Ambrosio Final BAD-D is treated as an independent tomographic signal rather than being mathematically blended into ERSS.

NICE

The NICE pathway is evaluated separately using the required corneal tomography inputs and an auditable component assessment.

PS3

PS3 adds another independently visible risk pathway with procedure-aware interpretation of selected corneal, refractive and inter-eye findings.

From Pentacam images to an assessment report

The workflow starts with the required examination images and clinical inputs. Measurements are linked to defined source fields; missing or conflicting decision-critical data are identified for completion or confirmation. ERSS, Final BAD-D, CER-AI-adapted NICE and PS3 are then presented as independent pathways alongside separate procedure-safety checks.

The report documents the assessment, reasons, warnings and applicable planning information. It supports the surgeon's review rather than declaring that surgery is risk-free. PDF and Word reporting use the already-computed canonical report payload; the public website does not calculate a separate assessment.

Tissue-safety assessment remains separate

A reassuring ectasia-risk pathway does not automatically establish procedural safety. CER-AI separately evaluates treatment-related tissue constraints, including pachymetry, anticipated ablation, residual stromal bed or residual stromal tissue, planned refractive correction and postoperative keratometric safety.

Why the pathways remain independent

Independent presentation makes disagreement between risk systems visible. It also allows the clinician to identify which observation or missing input is responsible for a caution or stop decision instead of receiving only a single opaque composite score.

Frequently asked questions about CER-AI

Is CER-AI a single corneal ectasia score?

No. CER-AI keeps ERSS, BAD-D, NICE and PS3 independently visible and separately interpretable. Tissue and procedure-safety calculations are also presented separately rather than being hidden inside one proprietary score.

Which Pentacam images does a CER-AI assessment require?

The current bilateral workflow requires five Pentacam sources: 4 Maps Refractive for OD and OS, Belin/Ambrósio BAD Display for OD and OS, and one Show 2 Exams Topometric comparison. A treatment card is optional; when it is absent or unreadable, the surgeon supplies the required manifest and intended refraction.

Who is CER-AI designed for?

CER-AI is intended for qualified ophthalmologists and refractive surgeons performing structured preoperative screening. It is not a patient self-assessment tool.

How does CER-AI handle missing or unreadable information?

Decision-critical values remain linked to defined source fields. When a required value cannot be obtained reliably, the workflow identifies the missing field for surgeon completion instead of silently inventing a value.

Does CER-AI decide whether surgery should be performed?

No. CER-AI organizes evidence, applies predefined assessment and safety rules, and documents the reasoning. The final clinical and surgical decision remains the surgeon's responsibility.

Has the complete CER-AI software been externally validated?

The public evidence pages identify literature supporting individual clinical concepts and risk pathways. Those publications do not constitute external validation of CER-AI as a complete software product. CER-AI does not claim validated diagnostic sensitivity, specificity or superiority unless supporting CER-AI-specific evidence is explicitly published.

Corneal ectasia diagnosis and the role of CER-AI

Corneal ectasia diagnosis is a clinical determination made by an ophthalmologist. CER-AI supports review of Pentacam topography and tomography reports and organizes ectasia risk findings for the surgeon. Preoperative risk screening is not the same as diagnosing established disease. CER-AI does not provide an autonomous diagnosis or replace clinical examination and assessment of progression.

How a CER-AI assessment works

  1. Upload the OD and OS 4 Maps Refractive and BAD Display images, plus one Show 2 Exams Topometric comparison.
  2. Review the source measurements; the surgeon completes required missing or unreadable fields.
  3. Inspect ERSS, BAD-D, NICE and PS3 independently alongside the tissue-safety calculations.
  4. Review the explanation and report. The final clinical and surgical decision remains with the surgeon.
